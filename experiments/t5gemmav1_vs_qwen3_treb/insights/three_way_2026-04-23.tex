\section*{TL;DR}

\textbf{Pretraining quality swamps architecture at the 2--5B scale.} We ran two
Gemma-2-lineage siblings --- encoder-decoder T5Gemma v1 vs pure decoder-only
Gemma-2-2B-IT (same base weights, same SFT/RLHF recipe, only architecture
differs) --- and a contemporaneous decoder-only from a different lineage
(Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) on 248 stratified TReB English/TCoT samples.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrlrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Variant} & \textbf{Params} & \textbf{Arch} & \textbf{Released} & \textbf{Mean} & \textbf{CORRECT\%} & \textbf{Sole wins} \\
\midrule
T5Gemma v1 2B-2B UL2-IT & 5.6\,B & enc-dec & Jun 2025 & 4.10 & 32.3\% & 15 \\
Gemma-2-2B-IT           & 2.6\,B & dec-only & Jul 2024 & 3.81 & 30.2\% & 13 \\
\rowcolor{hilite}
\textbf{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507} & 4.0\,B & dec-only & Aug 2025 & \textbf{4.99} & \textbf{33.1\%} & \textbf{27} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Within the Gemma-2 lineage, encoder-decoder roughly ties decoder-only.}
        T5Gemma edges Gemma-2-2B-IT by 0.29 on mean score and 2.1\,pp on CORRECT --- small.
        The clean architectural bump shows up only on the retrieval family
        (Table\_Query, Table\_Retrieval, Table\_Domain-Ops). Elsewhere enc-dec is a wash.
  \item \textbf{Qwen3-4B beats both}, despite being 30\% smaller than T5Gemma and
        from an entirely different pretraining culture. On 55 samples where exactly
        one model got CORRECT, Qwen3 claims 27 (49\%), T5Gemma 15 (27\%), Gemma-2 13 (24\%).
  \item \textbf{Qwen3's 45.6\% TRUNCATED rate} is a chain-of-thought-length artifact,
        not reasoning failure. Against our 256-token cap, Qwen3's verbose reasoning
        hits the ceiling almost half the time --- yet it still wins overall. Raising
        to 1024 tokens would likely widen the lead further.
  \item \textbf{47.6\% of samples are all-3-wrong} --- the 2--5B-class ceiling on TReB
        at TCoT difficulty; architecture choice doesn't move it.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{T5Gemma v1 is not a from-scratch encoder-decoder.} Per the v1 tech
report (arXiv:2504.06225), Google took the Gemma-2-2B pretrained
decoder, duplicated \& re-initialized it as an encoder, and adapted the
pair with UL2 denoising objectives, then ran their usual SFT + RLHF +
model-merging IT pipeline. So the T5Gemma v1 vs Gemma-2-2B-IT pair is
\emph{the same pretraining + the same post-training}, separated only by
``bolt on an encoder and do the UL2 adapt.'' Qwen3-4B is a separate
lineage entirely --- apples-vs-oranges on pretraining, but it's the
contemporaneous dec-only of comparable size so it's the obvious sanity
check.

\section{Setup}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Samples}: 248 stratified English/TCoT samples from TReB, pre-filtered
        to prompts $\le$ 4000 T5Gemma tokens (SWA-bug gate). All three variants
        evaluated on the same 248 sample ids.
  \item \textbf{Judge}: Claude Sonnet 4.6 via parallel Claude Code subagents,
        12-mode failure taxonomy (CORRECT, PARTIAL, FORMAT, HALLUCINATION,
        WRONG\_SELECTION, NUMERIC, REASONING, REFUSAL, EMPTY, MISUNDERSTAND,
        TRUNCATED, OTHER).
  \item \textbf{Generation}: bf16, greedy, \verb|max_new_tokens=256| for all three.
  \item \textbf{Backend}: T5Gemma via HF \verb|AutoModelForSeq2SeqLM|, Gemma-2
        and Qwen3 via HF \verb|AutoModelForCausalLM|. No vLLM (contested-GPU issues).
\end{itemize}

\section{What this says about encoder-decoder architecture}

Against the \emph{cleanest possible architectural comparator}
(Gemma-2-2B-IT --- same pretraining base, same IT recipe), T5Gemma v1's
encoder-decoder structure:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Wins the retrieval family} (Table\_Retrieval +1.7,
        Table\_Query +1.5, Table\_Domain-Ops +1.7 on mean score).
        Mechanistically consistent with cross-attention: the decoder can
        re-attend to the encoded table at every generation step.
  \item \textbf{Ties or loses everywhere else.} Mean score +0.29, CORRECT\%
        +2.1\,pp --- nothing to write home about when you're paying 2$\times$ the
        parameters for it.
\end{itemize}

Against the \emph{newer, different-lineage comparator}
(Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507), the architectural edge is invisible. Qwen3
beats both Gemma variants on mean score and CORRECT\%, and does so with
30\% fewer params than T5Gemma. That tells us pretraining + post-training
quality is more important than architecture at this parameter scale.

\section{Recommended follow-ups}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Rerun Qwen3-4B at \texttt{max\_new\_tokens=1024}} --- would likely
        convert a big chunk of its 113 TRUNCATED outcomes into CORRECT. Cheap ($\sim$\$0.50).
  \item \textbf{Qwen3-14B} for same-lineage scaling comparison, to see if the
        47.6\% all-wrong bucket is a 2--5B scale ceiling or a benchmark-difficulty floor.
  \item \textbf{Gemma-2-9B-IT} as a param-equal Gemma-2 decoder-only (closer to
        T5Gemma's 5.6B total params). Would sharpen the enc-dec-vs-dec-only
        comparison within Gemma lineage.
  \item \textbf{Relax the 4K-token filter} if T5Gemma v1's SWA bug doesn't
        trigger above 4094 --- would recover the Multi-step\_* family that's
        currently reduced to $n{=}1$.
\end{enumerate}

Sonnet judge pass: free (parallel Claude Code subagents). $\sim$12\,min of
wall-clock judge time for 744 judgments.

\section*{Appendix --- 5 illustrative examples}

\subsection*{(1) T5Gemma's encoder-decoder wins a retrieval task}

\textbf{Task}: Table\_Title\_Naming \quad \textbf{id}: \texttt{feeb1c38...}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.13\linewidth}p{0.80\linewidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{field} & \textbf{content} \\
\midrule
Q    & ``Please provide a title for the table based on its content.'' (table: 2014 ICC World Cricket League Division Four stats) \\
Gold & \texttt{The title is 2014 ICC World Cricket League Division Four} \\
\rowcolor{hilite}
\textbf{T5Gemma} & \textbf{CORRECT (10)} --- \texttt{\{"answer": "2014 ICC World Cricket League Division Four"\}} --- exact match \\
Gemma-2 & PARTIAL (8) --- \texttt{\{"answer": "...Division Four Information"\}} --- appended ``Information'' \\
Qwen3 & PARTIAL (8) --- \texttt{\{"answer": "...Division Four: Event Details"\}} --- appended ``: Event Details'' \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

All three see the table content, but T5Gemma commits to the exact gold
phrasing. Gemma-2 and Qwen3 both add descriptive suffixes --- a subtle
behavior that costs 2 points.

\subsection*{(2) Qwen3 wins a task needing semantic vocabulary}

\textbf{Task}: Table\_Query \quad \textbf{id}: \texttt{0e92a63b...}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.13\linewidth}p{0.80\linewidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{field} & \textbf{content} \\
\midrule
Q    & \texttt{which column is named "OT" in the table?} --- gold expects \emph{column index} \\
Gold & \texttt{This column is the 2th one.} (number\_answer: 2) \\
T5Gemma & PARTIAL --- \texttt{\{"answer": "OT"\}} --- answered the column \emph{name} not the index \\
Gemma-2 & WRONG\_SELECTION --- same trap, answered \texttt{"OT"} \\
\rowcolor{hilite}
\textbf{Qwen3} & \textbf{CORRECT} --- \texttt{\{"answer": "second"\}} --- correctly reads ``which column'' as positional \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both Gemmas parse the question the same (wrong) way; Qwen3's
instruction-following catches that ``which column is named X'' means
``give me the ordinal.''

\textbf{Task}: Table\_Fact\_Checking \quad \textbf{id}: \texttt{1fb344ea...}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.13\linewidth}p{0.80\linewidth}}

\midrule

\subsection*{(4) All 3 fail at the 2--5B ceiling}

\textbf{Task}: Table\_Correlation\_Analysis \quad \textbf{id}: \texttt{5ec3400a...}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.13\linewidth}p{0.80\linewidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{field} & \textbf{content} \\
\midrule
Q    & ``What is the correlation between the `number of branches' and `total assets' for a bank? Provide the correlation coefficient as evidence.'' \\
