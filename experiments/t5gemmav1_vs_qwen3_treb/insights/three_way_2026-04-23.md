# Three-way comparison: T5Gemma v1 vs Gemma-2-2B-IT vs Qwen3-4B — 2026-04-23

## TL;DR

**Pretraining quality swamps architecture at the 2–5B scale.** We ran two
Gemma-2-lineage siblings (encoder-decoder T5Gemma v1 vs pure decoder-only
Gemma-2-2B-IT — same base weights, same SFT/RLHF recipe, only architecture
differs) and a contemporaneous decoder-only from a different lineage
(Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) on 248 stratified TReB English/TCoT samples.

| Variant | Params | Arch | Released | Mean | CORRECT% | Sole wins |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| T5Gemma v1 2B-2B UL2-IT | 5.6B | enc-dec | **Jun 2025** | 4.10 | 32.3% | 15 |
| Gemma-2-2B-IT | 2.6B | dec-only | **Jul 2024** | 3.81 | 30.2% | 13 |
| **Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507** | 4.0B | dec-only | **Aug 2025** | **4.99** | **33.1%** | **27** |

1. **Within the Gemma-2 lineage, encoder-decoder roughly ties decoder-only.**
   T5Gemma v1 edges Gemma-2-2B-IT by 0.29 on mean score and 2.1 pp on
   CORRECT — small. The clean architectural bump shows up only on the
   retrieval family (Table_Query, Table_Retrieval, Table_Domain-Ops).
   Elsewhere enc-dec is a wash.
2. **Qwen3-4B beats both**, despite being 30% smaller than T5Gemma and
   coming from an entirely different pretraining culture. On the 55
   samples where exactly one model got CORRECT, Qwen3 claims 27 (49%),
   T5Gemma 15 (27%), Gemma-2 13 (24%) — Qwen3 is ~2× more likely than
   either Gemma to be the unique solver.
3. **Qwen3's 45.6% TRUNCATED rate is a chain-of-thought-length artifact**,
   not reasoning failure. Against our 256-token generation cap, Qwen3's
   verbose reasoning style hits the ceiling almost half the time — yet
   it still wins overall. Raising to 1024 tokens would likely widen the
   lead further.
4. **47.6% of samples are all-3-wrong** — this is the 2–5B-class ceiling
   on TReB at TCoT difficulty; architecture choice doesn't move it.

## Release timeline & lineage

```
Jul 2024 ────────┬──────────── Jun 2025 ── Aug 2025 ─────────
                 │                  │            │
            Gemma-2-2B-IT      T5Gemma v1    Qwen3-4B-
            (Google)           2B-2B         Instruct-2507
            dec-only, 2.6B     UL2-IT        (Alibaba)
                 │             (Google)      dec-only, 4.0B
                 │              5.6B         different lineage
                 └──►────[adapt+UL2 objective+─►◄── fresh pretraining
                         retrain encoder]─┐
                                          │
                                     enc-dec, 5.6B
```

**T5Gemma v1 is not a from-scratch encoder-decoder.** Per the v1 tech
report (arxiv:2504.06225), Google took the Gemma-2-2B pretrained
decoder, duplicated + re-initialized it as an encoder, and adapted the
pair with UL2 denoising objectives, then ran their usual SFT + RLHF +
model-merging IT pipeline. So the T5Gemma-v1-vs-Gemma-2-2B-IT pair is
**the same pretraining + the same post-training**, separated only by
"bolt on an encoder and do the UL2 adapt." Qwen3-4B is a separate
lineage entirely — apples-vs-oranges on pretraining, but it's the
contemporaneous dec-only of comparable size so it's the obvious sanity
check.

## Setup

- **Samples**: 248 stratified English/TCoT samples from TReB, pre-filtered
  to prompts ≤4000 T5Gemma tokens (SWA-bug gate). All three variants
  evaluated on the **same 248 sample ids**.
- **Judge**: Claude Sonnet 4.6 via parallel Claude Code subagents, 12-mode
  failure taxonomy (CORRECT / PARTIAL / FORMAT / HALLUCINATION /
  WRONG_SELECTION / NUMERIC / REASONING / REFUSAL / EMPTY /
  MISUNDERSTAND / TRUNCATED / OTHER).
- **Generation**: bf16, greedy, `max_new_tokens=256` for all three.
- **Backend**: T5Gemma via HF AutoModelForSeq2SeqLM, Gemma-2 and Qwen3
  via HF AutoModelForCausalLM. No vLLM (contested-GPU issues).

- **Table Understanding (73 samples)** is where the Gemma-2 lineage earns
  its keep. T5G sole wins 6 + T5G+G2-but-not-Qwen wins 12 = **18 of 73

  where Qwen3's verbose reasoning gets truncated mid-thought.
- **Table Basic Ops (35 samples)** is Qwen3's domain: 9 sole wins to 1

- **Data Analysis (18 samples)** is 15/18 all-wrong — nobody at this

more than either sibling).

## What this says about encoder-decoder architecture

Against the **cleanest possible architectural comparator** (Gemma-2-2B-IT
— same pretraining base, same IT recipe), T5Gemma v1's encoder-decoder
structure:

- **Wins the retrieval family** (Table_Retrieval +1.7, Table_Query +1.5,
  Table_Domain-Ops +1.7 on mean score). Mechanistically consistent with
  cross-attention: the decoder can re-attend to the encoded table at
  every generation step.
- **Ties or loses everywhere else.** Mean score +0.29, CORRECT% +2.1pp —
  nothing to write home about when you're paying 2× the parameters for
  it.

Against the **newer, different-lineage comparator** (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-
2507), the architectural edge is invisible. Qwen3 beats both Gemma
variants on mean score and CORRECT%, and does so with 30% fewer params
than T5Gemma. That tells us pretraining + post-training quality is
more important than architecture at this parameter scale.

## Recommended follow-ups

1. **Rerun Qwen3-4B at `max_new_tokens=1024`** — would likely convert a
   big chunk of its 113 TRUNCATED outcomes into CORRECT. Cheap (~$0.50).
2. **Qwen3-14B** for the same-lineage scaling comparison, to see if
   the 47.6% all-wrong bucket is a 2–5B scale ceiling or a benchmark-
   difficulty floor.
3. **Gemma-2-9B-IT** as a param-equal Gemma-2 decoder-only (closer to
   T5Gemma's 5.6B total params). Would sharpen the enc-dec-vs-dec-only
   comparison within Gemma lineage.
4. **Relax the 4K-token filter** if T5Gemma v1's SWA bug doesn't
   trigger above 4094 — would recover the Multi-step_* family that's
   currently reduced to n=1.

## Appendix — 5 illustrative examples

### (1) T5Gemma's encoder-decoder wins a retrieval task

**Task**: Table_Title_Naming · `id=feeb1c38...`

| field | content |
|---|---|
| Q | "Please provide a title for the table based on its content." (table: 2014 ICC World Cricket League Division Four stats) |
| Gold | `The title is 2014 ICC World Cricket League Division Four` |
| **T5Gemma** | **CORRECT (10)** — `{"answer": "2014 ICC World Cricket League Division Four"}` — exact match |
| Gemma-2 | PARTIAL (8) — `{"answer": "2014 ICC World Cricket League Division Four Information"}` — appended "Information" |
| Qwen3 | PARTIAL (8) — `{"answer": "2014 ICC World Cricket League Division Four: Event Details"}` — appended ": Event Details" |

All three see the table content, but T5Gemma commits to the exact gold
phrasing. The Gemmas-wait-Qwen both add descriptive suffixes — a
subtle behavior that costs 2 points.

### (2) Qwen3 wins a task needing semantic vocabulary

**Task**: Table_Query · `id=0e92a63b...`

| field | content |
|---|---|
| Q | `which column is named "OT" in the table?` — gold expects **column index** |
| Gold | `This column is the 2th one.` (number_answer: 2) |
| T5Gemma | PARTIAL — `{"answer": "OT"}` — answered the column *name* not the index |
| Gemma-2 | WRONG_SELECTION — same trap, answered `"OT"` |
| **Qwen3** | **CORRECT** — `{"answer": "second"}` — correctly reads "which column" as positional |

Both Gemmas parse the question the same (wrong) way; Qwen3's
instruction-following catches that "which column is named X" means
"give me the ordinal." Small but decisive difference.

### (3) Qwen3 truncation casualty — had the answer, ran out of tokens

**Task**: Table_Fact_Checking · `id=1fb344ea...`

| field | content |
|---|---|
| Q | `the model with 90% after 1 year have 80% after 3 year` — fact-check Entailment vs Contradiction |
| Gold | `Entailment` |
| T5Gemma | CORRECT — identifies hr-3utgb meets criteria, outputs Entailment |
| Gemma-2 | CORRECT — same logic, Entailment |
| Qwen3 | **TRUNCATED** — *reasoning* identifies hr-3utgb at 90%/80%, supports Entailment, **but generation stops before emitting `{"answer": ...}`** |

Qwen3 had the right answer but burned 256 tokens on reasoning. This is
the 113/248-sample pattern that a larger `max_new_tokens` would fix.

### (4) All 3 fail at the 2-5B ceiling

**Task**: Table_Correlation_Analysis · `id=5ec3400a...`

| field | content |
|---|---|
| Q | `What is the correlation between the 'number of branches' and 'total assets' for a bank? Provide the correlation coefficient as evidence.` |
| Gold | `Strong positive correlation, 0.97` |
| T5Gemma | FORMAT — explains Pearson formula methodology, never computes the number |
| Gemma-2 | TRUNCATED — cuts off mid-formula |
| Qwen3 | TRUNCATED — lists variable names, starts Pearson formula, cuts off before computing |

All three know the formula. None can actually execute the arithmetic
over tabular data and emit the coefficient. This is a representative
47.6%-all-wrong sample: the benchmark's Data Analysis family is above
what this parameter class can do.

### (5) Gemma-2-2B-IT wins a classification task

**Task**: Robustness_Evaluation · `id=8f7d6ce7...`

| field | content |
|---|---|
| Q | NLI-style: (A) Dana Reeve widow of Christopher Reeve... vs (B) Dana Reeve... → entailment or not_entailment |
| Gold | `not_entailment` |
| T5Gemma | WRONG_SELECTION — returns `entailment` (wrong) |
| **Gemma-2** | **CORRECT** — `not_entailment` |
| Qwen3 | TRUNCATED — gets to "Step 4: Check if (A) entails (B):" and stops |

Gemma-2 is particularly strong on Robustness_Evaluation (NLI labels)
— it commits to a label efficiently. T5Gemma hedges toward entailment;
Qwen3 over-reasons itself out of an answer. Gemma-2 wins 5/17 of these
samples uniquely.
